# 🤖 AI Assistance

AI coding assistants change how teams write and review software, but they do not remove
engineering leadership accountability for quality, skills, or measurement. Treat them as
productivity amplifiers that need explicit review norms, clear quality expectations, and caution
about vanity metrics.

## Resources

📄 [DORA | Artificial Intelligence](https://dora.dev/ai/) by DORA  
DORA's AI research and guidance for leaders — useful when you want evidence-backed caution about
how AI assistance interacts with delivery performance and team practices.

📄 [Research: quantifying GitHub Copilot's impact on developer productivity and
happiness](https://github.blog/news-insights/research/research-quantifying-github-copilots-impact-on-developer-productivity-and-happiness/)
by Eirini Kalliamvakou et al.  
Research on measured productivity effects of Copilot — a reminder to separate perceived speed
from verified outcomes, and to instrument carefully.

📄 [Exploring Generative AI](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai.html) by Martin
Fowler  
A thoughtful engineering take on generative AI: where it helps, where judgement still matters,
and why teams need deliberate practices rather than unchecked enthusiasm.

🎥 [How to Make the Best of AI Programming
Assistants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XavrebMKH2A) by Dave Farley  
Practical guidance on using AI assistants without abandoning engineering discipline — review
norms, verification, and keeping humans accountable for design quality.

🎥 [Has This Report EXPOSED THE TRUTH About AI Assisted Software
Development?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CoGO6s7bS3A) by Dave Farley  
A leadership-oriented critique of AI productivity claims, useful when stakeholders push metrics
that ignore quality risk and skill atrophy.
