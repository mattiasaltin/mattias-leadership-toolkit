# 📣 Product Evangelism

> "We need teams of missionaries, not teams of mercenaries." — John Doerr

Product evangelism is the continuous work of sharing vision and purpose so people become
missionaries for the product — not mercenaries executing a feature list. It is storytelling,
internal alignment, and culture-building.

## Resources

📘 [Made to Stick: Why Some Ideas Survive and Others
Die](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/69242.Made_to_Stick) by Chip Heath and Dan Heath  
What makes ideas memorable — the craft behind vision stories that survive retelling across an
organisation.

📘 [The Storytelling Edge: How to Transform Your Business, Stop Screaming into the Void, and Make
People Love You](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/35448018-the-storytelling-edge) by Joe
Lazauskas and Shane Snow  
Practical storytelling techniques for building product culture internally and externally.

📘 [Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers
Love](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/35249663-inspired) by Marty Cagan  
Includes the leadership work of selling the vision and building teams that believe in the
problems they are solving. Canonical home remains
[🌟 Product Vision](product-vision.md); listed here for the evangelism chapters.

📄 [The Most Important Thing](https://svpg.com/the-most-important-thing/) by Marty Cagan  
Argues that strong product vision — and the leadership work of communicating it — is the most
important product leadership responsibility.

🎥 [Mercenaries and Missionaries - John Doerr,
KPCB](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ui8yLqCxChM) by John Doerr  
The missionary vs mercenary framing that product leaders still use when describing culture and
motivation.

🎥 [Webinar: Mastering the Art of Storytelling as a PM by Uber Product Leader, Munawwar
Tayob](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwWq6KfsjXU) by Munawwar Tayob  
A concrete Product School session on making product narratives land with non-product audiences.
